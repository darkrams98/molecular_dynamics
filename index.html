What MD actually computes

Molecular dynamics solves Newton's equations for a few hundred thousand atoms. The output is not a picture — it is a trajectory, a time series of coordinates from which you take statistical averages.

The loop, in five lines

  1. Assign starting coordinates (from a PDB) and random velocities from a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution at temperature T.
  2. Compute the potential energy V of the whole system from the force field.
  3. Take the negative gradient: F = −∇V, so every atom gets a force, hence an acceleration.
  4. Integrate forward by Δt (typically 2 fs) to get new positions and velocities.
  5. Every n steps, write coordinates and energies to disk. Go to 2.

A 100 ns simulation with Δt = 2 fs is 50 million passes through that loop. Roughly 90 % of the wall-clock time is spent in step 2–3, and almost all of that is non-bonded interactions. Every performance trick in GROMACS is aimed there.

What you actually learn from a trajectory

structure

Conformational change

Loop motions, domain hinging, gate opening, helix unwinding — anything with a barrier low enough to cross in your simulated time.

energetics

Free energies

Binding affinities, permeation barriers, pKa shifts — via enhanced sampling, not plain MD.

interaction

Contacts & solvation

Which residue touches which lipid, how long a hydrogen bond lives, where ions sit in a pore.

transport

Kinetics & diffusion

Diffusion coefficients, permeation events, residence times — if you have enough independent replicas.

validation

Model quality

Does your homology model or cryo-EM loop stay folded? Instability under MD is itself a result.

prediction

Testable hypotheses

Mutate in silico, re-run, compare. Cheap relative to a wet-lab mutant.

The two failure modes Every criticism of an MD paper reduces to one of these. Force-field error: your potential energy function is an approximation, so the free-energy landscape is slightly wrong. Sampling error: you simulated 100 ns of a process that takes 100 µs, so your averages are from one corner of the landscape. Sampling error is usually the bigger one, and it is the one reviewers catch. Three independent 200 ns replicas beat one 600 ns run.

Force fields & water models

A force field is two things: a functional form for the potential energy, and a parameter set that fills in the constants. Choosing one is the single most consequential decision in your project.

The functional form

Almost every biomolecular force field is this sum. Learn to recognise each term in a GROMACS energy table:

V(r) — additive class I force field
V  =  Σ_bonds   k_b (r − r0)²            bond stretching       (harmonic spring)
   +  Σ_angles  k_θ (θ − θ0)²            angle bending
   +  Σ_UB      k_UB (s − s0)²           Urey-Bradley 1-3 term  (CHARMM only)
   +  Σ_dihed   k_φ [1 + cos(nφ − δ)]    proper torsions        (periodic)
   +  Σ_improp  k_ξ (ξ − ξ0)²            impropers: planarity, chirality
   +  CMAP                               φ/ψ cross-term grid    (CHARMM36, ff19SB)
   +  Σ_pairs   4ε[(σ/r)¹² − (σ/r)⁶]     van der Waals          (Lennard-Jones 12-6)
   +  Σ_pairs   q_i q_j / (4πε0 r)       electrostatics         (fixed point charges)

The first six terms are bonded and cheap: they involve a fixed list of 2–4 atoms. The last two are non-bonded, scale as N², and consume your compute budget. Atoms 1–2 and 1–3 along a bond path are excluded from non-bonded terms; 1–4 pairs are included but scaled down (AMBER: 1/1.2 electrostatic, 1/2.0 LJ; CHARMM: uses dedicated 1-4 parameters).

Combination rules

LJ parameters exist per atom type; pair parameters are generated on the fly. GROMACS comb-rule in the [ defaults ] block: rule 2 (Lorentz–Berthelot, σ arithmetic / ε geometric) for CHARMM and AMBER, rule 3 (geometric for both) for OPLS. Getting this wrong silently produces a different force field.

Lennard-Jones potential & force — drag to see what a cutoff really does V(r)F(r)cutoff

Default values are roughly an aliphatic carbon. Note the residual jump at rc with a plain cutoff: that discontinuity injects energy every time a pair crosses the boundary, which is why GROMACS always applies a modifier. CHARMM36 was parameterised with force-switching between 1.0 and 1.2 nm, so using potential-shift with it changes the lipid properties you are trying to reproduce.

Where the parameters physically live in GROMACSdeep dive

A force field in GROMACS is a directory named charmm36-jul2022.ff/ sitting either in $GMXDATA/top/ or in your working directory (local copies win). Inside:

  • forcefield.itp — the [ defaults ] block: combination rule, fudgeLJ/fudgeQQ, and includes.
  • ffnonbonded.itp / ffbonded.itp — atom types with σ, ε, and all bonded parameters.
  • aminoacids.rtp — the residue topology database. This is what pdb2gmx reads to decide which atoms a residue should have and what they connect to. "Residue X not found in residue topology database" means: not in here.
  • aminoacids.hdb — hydrogen database: how to add missing H atoms.
  • *.tdb — terminus database (NH3+, COO−, neutral caps).
  • residuetypes.dat — tells GROMACS which residues count as Protein, DNA, Water, Ion. Add your ligand here to make sensible default index groups.
  • tip3p.itp, ions.itp, spc216.gro — solvent and ion definitions and a pre-equilibrated water box.

Integrators, timestep & constraints

The integrator turns forces into motion. The timestep is limited by the fastest vibration in the system, and constraints exist to remove those fast motions so you can take bigger steps.

How big can Δt be?

Rule of thumb: you need roughly 10 integration steps per period of the fastest motion. A C–H stretch has a period near 10 fs, so 1 fs is safe if nothing is constrained.

Boxes, PBC, cutoffs & PME

A 60 000-atom droplet in vacuum is mostly surface. Periodic boundary conditions remove the surface by making the box tile space infinitely — which then forces a careful treatment of how far each atom is allowed to see.

Periodic boundary conditions

The simulation cell is replicated in all directions. An atom leaving the right face re-enters from the left, and every atom interacts with the nearest copy of every other atom — the minimum image convention. There is no wall, no surface tension artefact, and no evaporation.

minimum image convention — the central cell and its images
The rule that protects you The box must satisfy L > 2 rc in every direction, and the solute must never see its own image. In practice: leave at least 1.0–1.2 nm of solvent between the protein and every box face (editconf -d 1.2), and more if your protein can extend. Check it afterwards with gmx mindist -pi: the minimum periodic distance should stay comfortably above 2 rc for the whole trajectory. A protein that interacts with its own image produces beautiful, meaningless results.

Internally GROMACS represents every box as a triclinic matrix, so a dodecahedron is stored as a skewed box. This is why a snapshot in VMD can look like a sheared brick — that is correct, not corruption. Use gmx trjconv -ur compact -pbc mol to render it as the compact unit cell.

Cutoffs and the neighbour list

Van der Waals forces decay as r−6, so they are truncated at rvdw (typically 1.0–1.2 nm). To avoid rebuilding the pair list every step, GROMACS keeps a slightly larger list (rlist) and refreshes it every nstlist steps. Since the Verlet scheme (the only scheme since GROMACS 2020), you no longer set the buffer by hand: set verlet-buffer-tolerance (default 0.005 kJ/mol/ps per atom) and GROMACS computes rlist for you, and mdrun may even increase nstlist at runtime for GPU efficiency. Let it.

Electrostatics: why PME is not optional

Coulomb decays as r−1. Truncating it is a physical error, not an approximation — it produces artificial ordering of water and ions at the cutoff, and distorts charged side chains. Particle Mesh Ewald splits the sum:

  • Real space: a screened short-range part, computed pairwise up to rcoulomb.
  • Reciprocal space: the smooth long-range remainder, computed by mapping charges onto a 3-D grid (fourierspacing, interpolation order pme-order = 4) and doing a 3-D FFT.

Cost scales as N log N. On multi-rank runs, GROMACS can put PME on dedicated ranks (mdrun -npme) or on the GPU (-pme gpu).

Ensembles, thermostats & barostats

Newton's equations conserve energy, which is not what a wet lab does. Coupling algorithms bolt temperature and pressure control onto the integrator so that your averages correspond to a real thermodynamic ensemble.

The three ensembles you will use

microcanonical

NVE

Fixed atoms, volume, energy. No coupling at all. Its only routine use is a diagnostic: if total energy drifts in NVE, your timestep, cutoffs or constraints are wrong.

canonical

NVT

Fixed atoms, volume, temperature. Used for the first equilibration phase: bring the system to 300 K while the box is held still.

isothermal–isobaric

NPT

Fixed atoms, pressure, temperature. Second equilibration phase and nearly all production runs — it lets the density relax to the correct value.

Membrane work adds two variants: NPAT (fixed area per lipid) and NPγT (fixed surface tension). Modern lipid force fields are good enough that plain semi-isotropic NPT with zero surface tension is the standard choice.

Illustrative model, not a real simulation — but the qualitative lessons are real. Berendsen reaches the target quickly and then fluctuates too little (the distribution is wrong even though the mean looks perfect). Nosé–Hoover overshoots and rings, with the ringing period set by τ. And note how the fluctuation amplitude shrinks as 1/√Ndf: a ±10 K swing in a small box is normal, the same swing in a 200 000-atom system is not.

Coupling groups Use at most two temperature-coupling groups, and only ones that exchange energy well: tc-grps = Protein Non-Protein for a soluble protein, or tc-grps = Protein_Membrane Solvent_Ions for a bilayer. Coupling many small groups separately (each residue, each ion) creates the "hot solvent / cold solute" artefact, where the thermostat hides a real energy leak. Each group needs its own tau-t and ref-t value.

Minimisation, solvation & ions

Preparation is where most simulations are actually ruined. Each step here removes a specific, identifiable problem from the starting structure.

Energy minimisation — why, and how far

A crystal structure plus a randomly packed water box contains clashes: atoms at 0.05 nm separation where the LJ potential is astronomically steep. Feed that to the integrator and the first force calculation launches an atom across the box. Minimisation walks downhill on the potential energy surface until the maximum force falls below a threshold. It is not dynamics — there is no time, no temperature, no velocity.

minim.mdp
integrator = steep      ; steepest descent
emtol      = 1000.0     ; stop when max force < 1000 kJ/mol/nm
emstep     = 0.01       ; initial step size (nm)
nsteps     = 50000      ; upper bound; convergence usually comes first
  • Success looks like: Steepest Descents converged to Fmax < 1000 in N steps, and a large negative potential energy (order −105 to −106 kJ/mol for a solvated protein).
  • Trouble looks like: "did not converge to Fmax < 1000 in 50001 steps" with Fmax still in the 104–106 range, or a positive potential energy. Something is physically wrong — overlapping atoms, a bad ligand topology, a duplicated chain.
emtol in practice1000 kJ/mol/nm is fine before dynamics. Only push to 10 or 1 if you are doing normal-mode analysis or a QM-style single point. Minimising harder does not make your MD better.

Solvation

gmx solvate stacks a pre-equilibrated solvent box (spc216.gro for any 3-site water; tip4p.gro for 4-site) over your box, deletes any molecule that overlaps the solute using van der Waals radii, and appends the count to your topology. Expect roughly 10 water molecules per protein residue for a 1.0 nm shell — a 130-residue protein in a 1.0 nm dodecahedral box lands near 20 000–25 000 atoms total.

Ions: neutralisation and ionic strength

PME requires a neutral cell. A net charge is silently neutralised by a uniform background charge, which introduces artefacts around charged groups. Two things to add:

  1. Counter-ions to bring the net charge to exactly zero (genion -neutral).
  2. Bulk salt to a physiological concentration — -conc 0.15 for 150 mM NaCl, or whatever your experiment used. Simulating a channel in pure water is a modelling error, not a simplification.
terminal
# build a .tpr just so genion knows the topology, then swap waters for ions
gmx grompp -f ions.mdp -c solv.gro -p topol.top -o ions.tpr -maxwarn 1
gmx genion -s ions.tpr -o solv_ions.gro -p topol.top \
           -pname NA -nname CL -neutral -conc 0.15
# when prompted, choose group 'SOL' — ions replace water molecules
Common mistakegenion edits topol.top in place, decrementing SOL and adding NA/CL lines. If you run it twice, or re-run solvate afterwards, your topology and coordinate file disagree and grompp fails with "number of coordinates in coordinate file does not match topology". Keep a clean copy of the topology before you start.

Position restraints and staged equilibration

pdb2gmx writes posre.itp, a harmonic tether (default 1000 kJ mol−1 nm−2) on every heavy atom, wrapped in the topology by an #ifdef POSRES block. Activating it with define = -DPOSRES holds the solute still while water and ions relax around it.

grompp with restraints — the -r flag is mandatory
gmx grompp -f nvt.mdp -c em.gro -r em.gro -p topol.top -o nvt.tpr

-r supplies the reference coordinates the restraints pull toward. Since GROMACS 2018 it is required whenever POSRES is defined; omitting it is a fatal error, and pointing it at the wrong file silently drags your protein toward a different conformation.

Release restraints graduallyFor anything delicate — a membrane protein, a modelled loop, a docked ligand — do not jump from 1000 to 0. Run a short NPT stage at each of 1000 → 500 → 200 → 50 → 0 kJ mol−1 nm−2. CHARMM-GUI generates exactly this ladder (six stages) for you. It costs a few nanoseconds and saves days of debugging.

GROMACS vs the other engines

All major engines integrate the same equations with the same force fields. They differ in what they make easy, what they make fast, and what they cost. This guide teaches GROMACS — here is why, and when you should reach for something else.

The short answer

use gromacs when

You want throughput on biomolecules

Protein, membrane, nucleic acid systems of 104–106 atoms; free and unrestricted; the fastest single-node GPU performance in its class; and the largest built-in analysis toolbox of any engine.

use something else when

You need a specialised capability

Reactive or materials potentials → LAMMPS. Custom forces and ML potentials from Python → OpenMM. Alchemical free energy with the deepest tooling → AMBER. Machines with 10 000+ cores on one system → NAMD.

Rough performance intuition — read the caveatbenchmarking

For a ~100 000-atom solvated membrane protein, 2 fs timestep, PME, on one modern workstation GPU plus a decent CPU, all the GPU-capable engines land in the same order of magnitude: tens of ns/day. GROMACS and AMBER pmemd.cuda typically lead on a single GPU; NAMD pulls ahead once you scale across many nodes; OpenMM sits close behind on one GPU.

Do not trust any number you did not measure, including that one. Performance depends on GPU model, CPU core count, whether PME and bonded forces are offloaded, nstlist, box shape, and constraint settings. Always benchmark your system for 5 minutes before committing a month of cluster time — see section 17.

GROMACS in ten minutes

GROMACS is one binary, gmx, with about a hundred subcommands. Once you have the mental model, the rest is vocabulary.

The mental model: a compiler and an executable

the compiler

gmx grompp

Takes your settings (.mdp), coordinates (.gro), and topology (.top), checks that they are mutually consistent, resolves every parameter, and writes one self-contained binary: .tpr. Almost every setup error is caught here.

the executable

gmx mdrun

Takes the .tpr and runs it. It needs nothing else — you can copy a single .tpr to a cluster on the other side of the world and reproduce the run bit-for-bit on the same version. That portability is the whole point of the format.

the two-command core of every simulation
gmx grompp -f md.mdp -c npt.gro -t npt.cpt -p topol.top -o md.tpr
gmx mdrun  -deffnm md

Group selection: the prompt everyone gets stuck on

Many analysis tools stop and ask which atoms to use. Default groups (System, Protein, Backbone, C-alpha, SOL, Water_and_ions…) come from residuetypes.dat. You answer with a number or a name.

Your main trajectory. Positions only, compressed with the XDR/xdrfile algorithm — roughly 3–10× smaller than .trr. The default precision of 1000 (0.001 nm) is finer than any structural conclusion you will draw.

restarting and continuing
# resume an interrupted run — appends to existing xtc/edr/log, bitwise continuation
gmx mdrun -deffnm md -cpi md.cpt

# start a new stage from the end of the previous one (velocities + coupling carried over)
gmx grompp -f md.mdp -c npt.gro -t npt.cpt -p topol.top -o md.tpr

# extend a completed run by another 50 ns
gmx convert-tpr -s md.tpr -extend 50000 -o md_ext.tpr

# write checkpoints more often on an unreliable queue (minutes)
gmx mdrun -deffnm md -cpt 5

trjconv, the tool that confuses everyone

Every trajectory needs post-processing before analysis, because molecules are stored wrapped into the box. A protein that drifts across a boundary appears to explode. Fix it in this order — and never skip the first step, because centring a broken molecule centres it wrongly.

the canonical three-pass PBC recipe
# 1. make molecules whole again (choose System)
gmx trjconv -s md.tpr -f md.xtc -o whole.xtc -pbc whole

# 2. centre on the protein and use the compact unit cell (choose Protein, then System)
gmx trjconv -s md.tpr -f whole.xtc -o center.xtc -pbc mol -center -ur compact

# 3. remove global rotation and translation (choose Backbone, then System)
gmx trjconv -s md.tpr -f center.xtc -o fit.xtc -fit rot+trans

# bonus: a clean starting frame to load into VMD as the topology
gmx trjconv -s md.tpr -f fit.xtc -o first.gro -dump 0

Errors you will hit at this steptroubleshooting
  • "Residue 'XXX' not found in residue topology database" — a ligand, a modified residue, or a crystallisation additive. Either delete it or build parameters for it (section 14).
  • "Atom H is missing in residue LYS 1" — the PDB has non-standard or partial hydrogen naming. Add -ignh so GROMACS deletes all hydrogens and rebuilds them from its own database.
  • "Long bond" / "Bond of length X between atoms" — usually a chain break from missing residues. Model the loop (MODELLER, SWISS-MODEL, AlphaFold) or split the chain and cap the termini.
  • Wrong protonation — pdb2gmx defaults to neutral pH forms and picks histidine tautomers by hydrogen-bond geometry. For any mechanistic question, set them deliberately with -his, informed by PROPKA or H++.

Membrane proteins & CHARMM-GUI

A bilayer changes almost every setting: the box is anisotropic, pressure coupling must be semi-isotropic, equilibration takes ten times longer, and the lipids need their own validation. This is the workflow that works.

Why membrane systems are harder

  • Two phases, two relaxation times. Water equilibrates in picoseconds; lipid packing and area per lipid take tens to hundreds of nanoseconds. Your protein RMSD can look converged while the bilayer around it is still settling.
  • Anisotropy. The membrane normal (z) is physically distinct from the membrane plane (x–y), so pcoupltype = semiisotropic is mandatory. Isotropic coupling squeezes the bilayer and gives the wrong thickness.
  • Insertion artefacts. The protein has to be placed in a hole in the bilayer. Lipid tails end up threaded through loops, and water ends up in the hydrophobic core. Both must be fixed before dynamics, not during.
  • Size. A modest channel in a 10 × 10 nm patch with water slabs is 100 000–200 000 atoms — an order of magnitude more expensive than lysozyme.

Route 1 — CHARMM-GUI Membrane Builder (recommended)

Free, web-based, and it emits ready-to-run GROMACS inputs including a six-stage restraint-release ladder. Realistically this is the fastest correct path for anyone who is not developing methods.

Membrane failures and how to recognise themtroubleshooting
  • Water in the hydrophobic core at t = 0 — a build artefact. It usually leaves during the restrained ladder; if it does not, rebuild. Do not "fix" it by deleting waters mid-run.
  • A lipid tail threaded through a loop or the pore — visible in frame 0 in VMD. Rebuild with a different random seed or a larger insertion radius; it will never resolve itself.
  • Area per lipid drifting downward for 200 ns — not yet equilibrated. Membrane systems need 100–200 ns of unrestrained NPT before production begins; that time does not count as production.
  • The protein tilts progressively — either genuine (interesting) or a hydrophobic-mismatch artefact from a lipid whose thickness does not suit the protein. Compare against the OPM-predicted embedding.
  • Box x–y shrinking without bound — a patch too small for the protein, or isotropic coupling left on.
  • Ions stuck in the bilayer — genion placed them badly. Rebuild ion placement in the water slabs.

Practical details that bite

Principal component analysis and free-energy landscapes

PCA (essential dynamics) diagonalises the covariance matrix of atomic fluctuations. Honest caveatA free-energy landscape from a single unbiased 100 ns trajectory is a histogram of what you happened to sample, not a converged free-energy surface. Basins you never entered have infinite apparent free energy. Compute it on pooled replicas, check that separate replicas give the same landscape, and never quote a barrier height from unbiased MD — that is what umbrella sampling and metadynamics are for.

Error bars, and how to know when you are done

Successive frames of MD are strongly correlated, so the naive standard error over N frames underestimates the true uncertainty by an order of magnitude. Three defensible approaches:

within one run

Block averaging

gmx analyze -f obs.xvg -ee splits the series into blocks and extrapolates the block-size dependence. The plateau is the real error estimate.

across runs

Independent replicas

3–5 runs from different seeds. The spread between replica means is the most honest error bar there is, and the only one that captures sampling error.

diagnostic

Cosine content

Cosine content of the first PCs (gmx analyze -cc) near 1 means your "collective motion" is indistinguishable from random diffusion — a classic false positive.

Discard the beginningAlways drop the equilibration portion before averaging: gmx energy -f md.edr -b 20000, gmx rms ... -b 20000. Choose the cut-off by looking at the data (where does the observable stop drifting?), state it explicitly, and apply it consistently to every replica.

Umbrella sampling, the full protocol

The workhorse for a potential of mean force along a well-defined coordinate.

  1. Generate the pathway. A slow steered-MD pull along the reaction coordinate, writing frames often.
  2. Extract windows spaced ~0.1 nm apart along that coordinate — typically 20–40 windows.
  3. Run each window with a static harmonic restraint at its position, 10–50 ns each, discarding the first 10 %.
  4. Check overlap of neighbouring histograms. Gaps mean the PMF is unreliable there; add windows or raise the force constant.
  5. Combine with WHAM and bootstrap the errors.
steered pull, then windows, then WHAM

# 2. split into frames and pick windows ~0.1 nm apart from pullx.xvg
echo System | gmx trjconv -s pull.tpr -f pull.xtc -o conf.gro -sep

for i in $(seq 0 23); do
  gmx grompp -f umbrella.mdp -c conf${i}.gro -p topol.top -n index.ndx -o umb${i}.tpr
  gmx mdrun -deffnm umb${i} -pf umb${i}_f.xvg -px umb${i}_x.xvg

# 4. WHAM with 200 bootstrap resamples

Alchemical free energy

Turn a molecule's interactions off in a series of λ windows, in the binding site and in bulk water, and take the difference: a thermodynamic cycle gives the binding free energy. The .mdp block is in section 10. Practical rules:

  • Decouple charges first, then van der Waals — never simultaneously.
  • Always use soft-core (sc-alpha = 0.5) for the vdW legs, or the endpoint diverges.
  • Typically 11–21 λ windows, 5–20 ns each, with overlap checked between neighbours.
  • Set calc-lambda-neighbors = -1 to enable MBAR, which uses all the data rather than adjacent pairs.
  • Estimate with gmx bar for a quick BAR number, and with alchemlyb for MBAR plus proper convergence diagnostics.
Expectation settingRelative binding free energies for congeneric ligand series are reliable to roughly 1 kcal/mol when done carefully. Absolute binding free energies are much harder. MM/PBSA is fast and often useful for ranking, but its absolute values are not quantitative — treat it as a filter, not a measurement.

Performance & HPC

A badly configured GROMACS run can be three times slower than the same job configured well, on identical hardware. Five minutes of benchmarking is the highest-return thing you can do before a month-long campaign.

Where the time goes

For a typical solvated biomolecular system: short-range non-bonded ≈ 60 %, PME ≈ 20 %, bonded + constraints ≈ 10 %, neighbour search + domain decomposition ≈ 10 %. GPU offload targets the first two, which is why a single modern GPU can outrun dozens of CPU cores.

Error messages, decoded

GROMACS errors are unusually informative once you know the pattern. Almost all of them fall into three families: your topology disagrees with your coordinates, your system is physically broken, or you asked for a parallel layout that does not fit.

grompp refuses to build a .tpr

Residue 'LIG' not found in residue topology databasepdb2gmx

Means: there is no entry for this residue in the force field's .rtp file, so pdb2gmx cannot build its topology. Common culprits: ligands, cofactors, modified residues, sugars, crystallisation additives (GOL, EDO, SO4, PEG), non-standard ions.

Fix: delete it if it is an artefact of crystallisation; otherwise generate parameters (section 14). Confirm what it actually is first — grep HETATM input.pdb | awk '{print $4}' | sort | uniq -c.

Atom HG1 in residue SER 45 was not found in rtp entry SERpdb2gmx

Means: hydrogen naming in your PDB does not match the force field's convention, or an atom is genuinely missing.

Fix: add -ignh so pdb2gmx strips all hydrogens and rebuilds them from its own database. If a heavy atom is missing, no flag will save you: rebuild the side chain (PyMOL, Chimera, MODELLER) first.

number of coordinates in coordinate file (23198) does not match topology (23194)grompp

Means: the sum over [ molecules ] ≠ the atom count in your .gro. Nearly always caused by running solvate or genion twice against the same topology, or by editing a coordinate file without updating the counts.

Fix: compare the numbers. Line 2 of the .gro is the truth; adjust the [ molecules ] block to match, or restart preparation from a clean topology. Never paper over it with -maxwarn — this one is fatal for a reason.

Atomtype CG321 not found / Invalid order for directive defaultsgrompp

Means: your #include statements are in the wrong order. The force field must come first; any [ atomtypes ] block (typically a ligand's .prm) must follow it and precede every [ moleculetype ] that uses those types.

Fix: reorder as shown in section 14. A ligand include pasted at the bottom of the file, after the water, produces exactly this error.

WARNING: System has non-zero total charge: -8.000000grompp

Means: exactly what it says. Under PME a net charge is neutralised by a uniform background, which distorts energetics around charged groups.

Fix: run genion -neutral. Seeing this warning when building the ions.tpr is expected — that is the one legitimate -maxwarn 1. Seeing it afterwards means genion did not do what you thought.

You are using position restraints but you did not supply -rgrompp

Means: define = -DPOSRES is active but no reference structure was given.

Fix: add -r <file>.gro. Use the structure the restraints should pull toward — normally the input of the current stage, or (for CHARMM-GUI ladders) the originally built coordinates.

mdrun starts and then dies

LINCS WARNING: relative constraint deviation after LINCSmdrun

Means: the constraint solver could not satisfy a bond length — atoms moved too far in one step. It is a symptom, not the disease. A handful of warnings in the first picoseconds of a fresh equilibration can be survivable; a stream of them ending in a crash is not.

Diagnose in this order:

  1. Did minimisation actually converge? Check the Fmax line in em.log.
  2. Look at the step*.pdb file mdrun dumped — it contains the offending atoms. Open it in VMD and look at that region. The problem is usually visible.
  3. Is the ligand or non-standard residue topology correct? Bad parameters concentrate the failure in one molecule.
  4. Is Δt too large for your constraint settings?

Rescue sequence: re-minimise → run 100 ps of NVT with dt = 0.0005 and strong position restraints → step Δt back up. If it survives that, the start structure was strained; if it still dies, the topology is wrong.

Water molecule starting at atom 15234 can not be settledmdrun

Means: the same failure as a LINCS warning, but in the analytical water constraint solver. The water is being torn apart by an enormous force, so something adjacent to it has already exploded.

Fix: as above — but also check for a water molecule trapped inside the protein or inside the hydrophobic core of a bilayer, and for two solvent molecules placed on top of each other by a manual coordinate edit.

1-4 interaction between atoms 512 and 515 at distance 1.2e+04 exceeds the table limitmdrun

Means: the system has already blown up; two atoms bonded 1-4 are now kilometres apart. The listed atoms are where it was detected, not necessarily where it started.

Fix: treat as a blow-up. Almost always: no/failed minimisation, a wrong ligand topology, a bad -maxwarn override, or restarting a run with mismatched coordinates and velocities.

There is no domain decomposition for 16 ranks that is compatible with the given box and a minimum cell size of 1.4 nmmdrun

Means: your box cannot be cut into that many pieces while each piece stays larger than the cutoff plus the largest bonded interaction.

Fix, in order of preference: use fewer ranks and more OpenMP threads (-ntmpi 4 -ntomp 4 instead of -ntmpi 16); use a bigger box if it was too small anyway; or relax the constraint with -rdd / -dds only if you understand what you are trading away. A small system on many ranks is the underlying problem — run replicas instead.

Segmentation fault, or a step####.pdb appears with no other messagemdrun

Means: a hard crash, usually from the same instability as above, occasionally from a genuine hardware or build problem.

Fix: read the tail of md.log first — the last energy block usually shows the potential energy diverging over the preceding steps. If energies look fine and the crash is abrupt and irreproducible, suspect the node: run gmx mdrun -nsteps 5000 twice and compare, and try a different node or a CPU-only run.

The cut-off length is longer than half the shortest box vectorgrompp / mdrun

Means: the minimum image convention is violated — an atom can see two copies of the same neighbour.

Fix: rebuild with a bigger box (editconf -d 1.2 or more) and re-solvate. Reducing the cutoff to make the error go away is not a fix; it changes the force field.

This can also appear mid-run if the box shrinks — a sign the barostat is misconfigured or the system was never properly equilibrated.

Best practice & common mistakes

The ten mistakes that appear in most first projects

  1. One long run instead of several independent ones. 3 × 200 ns from different velocity seeds is scientifically stronger than 1 × 600 ns, and it finishes sooner on three GPUs.
  2. -maxwarn 10. Every warning is grompp telling you something specific. Suppressing them in bulk is how people end up with a neutralised-by-background-charge, wrong-cutoff, mismatched-topology simulation that runs happily for a month.
  3. Ignoring the PDB header. Missing residues, alternate conformations, engineered mutations and crystallisation additives are all documented in the file you downloaded.
  4. Default protonation everywhere. Histidine tautomers and the protonation state of catalytic residues are modelling decisions. Make them explicitly, with PROPKA or H++ as a starting point.
  5. Mixing force fields. A CHARMM lipid with an AMBER protein, or CHARMM36 with AMBER's cutoff scheme.
  6. Treating equilibration as production. Restrained stages and the first tens of nanoseconds are not data. For membranes, budget 100–200 ns before production starts.
  7. Analysing without fixing PBC. Every RMSD spike you cannot explain is this.
  8. Over-reading a single trajectory. One binding event in one replica is an anecdote. Report how many replicas showed it.
  9. Writing frames too rarely — or too often. Decide from the analysis you plan: 10 ps for structure, 1–2 ps for hydrogen-bond kinetics, 100 ps if you only need cluster representatives.
  10. No error bars. Block averaging or the spread across replicas. A mean with no uncertainty is not a result.

Habits worth building

provenance

Version-control the inputs

A git repository with every .mdp, the topology, the build script and a README recording the GROMACS version, the force field version string and the seeds. Trajectories go elsewhere; the recipe fits in kilobytes.

discipline

Read mdout.mdp once per stage

It is the resolved settings, including everything you did not specify. Diffing consecutive stages catches the parameter you forgot to change.

validation

Check the system before the science

Temperature, density, box volume, minimum periodic distance, and for membranes the area per lipid — all flat before you analyse anything biological.

visual

Watch the trajectory

Load it in VMD and scrub through. Thirty seconds of looking catches lipid tails through loops, ions stuck in the core, a broken chain, or a ligand that left in the first nanosecond.

scale

Test small, then scale

Run 100 ps of every new setup before submitting a week-long job. Most fatal problems appear in the first picoseconds.

honesty

Pre-register your analysis

Write down what you expect and what would refute it, before you look. It is remarkably easy to find a story in noise once you have 10 000 frames and a plotting library.

What a reviewer will look for in your methods

reproducibility checklist GROMACS version · force field and its version string (e.g. CHARMM36m, July 2022) · water model · box type and solute–box clearance · system size in atoms · ion type and concentration · integrator, timestep and constraint settings · cutoff scheme with all radii and modifiers · electrostatics method and grid spacing · thermostat and barostat with coupling groups and time constants · full equilibration protocol including restraint force constants and durations · production length · number of independent replicas · how much was discarded as equilibration · analysis tools and their parameters (cluster cut-offs, hydrogen-bond criteria, fit groups) · error estimation method · where the inputs and trajectories are deposited.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Collective variable (CV)A reduced coordinate describing the process of interest — a distance, an angle, a coordination number. What you bias in enhanced sampling
EnsembleThe set of thermodynamic constraints held fixed (NVE, NVT, NPT), which determines what your averages mean
EquilibrationThe initial part of a run during which observables are still relaxing toward their steady values. Not data
Force fieldThe functional form of the potential energy plus its parameters
Free energy landscape−kBT ln P over one or two coordinates; basins are stable states, ridges are barriers
Hydrogen mass repartitioning (HMR)Moving mass from heavy atoms onto their hydrogens to slow the fastest motions and permit a 4 fs timestep
Minimum image conventionEach atom interacts only with the nearest periodic copy of every other atom
PMEParticle Mesh Ewald: splits electrostatics into a short-range pairwise part and a grid-based long-range part
PMFPotential of mean force — the free energy as a function of a chosen coordinate
Position restraintA harmonic spring tethering an atom to a reference position. Not a constraint
ReplicaAn independent repetition of the same simulation with different initial velocities
RMSD / RMSFDeviation of a structure from a reference / fluctuation of each atom about its own mean
Soft-core potentialA modified LJ form used in alchemical transformations to avoid singularities as a particle vanishes
Thermostat / barostatAlgorithms that couple the system to a temperature or pressure bath
TrajectoryThe time-ordered sequence of coordinate frames — the raw output of MD
Verlet bufferThe extra shell added to the pair-list radius so no interaction is missed between list rebuilds
